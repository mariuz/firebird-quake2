        The page installs as an app and, once it has run, runs without a network ("offline ready"); a pak picked from the PAK control is kept in this browser and loads at the next start (the button beside it goes back to the demo's), and a picked music folder's tracks are kept as they play.
        On touch screens the left half of the view moves, the right half looks, a tap fires, and the buttons fire, jump, change weapon and open the menu.
        A gamepad works too: left stick moves, right stick looks, RT fires, A jumps, B crouches, LB/RB change weapon, the d-pad selects (left/right) and uses (up) an inventory item, Y shows the inventory, Back the help computer, Start opens the menu (the d-pad, A and B drive it).
        With two to four players (coop, or deathmatch: the maps' deathmatch weapons and spots, no monsters, frags at the top right, F1 the score board, with each player's ping over the network;
        dmflags, fraglimit, timelimit and cheats at the console) the view is split in two or four, the keyboard and mouse are the first player's and the gamepads the others' (640×480 gives four views of 320×240).
        Over the network: one page hosts (Host makes an offer to send to the other player), the other joins (Join takes the offer and makes an answer to send back),
        and the host's game runs with the guest's player in it (coop, or a deathmatch when the host's Players select says so), each page its own view; Host again while hosting makes an offer for a third and a fourth player, who join the game as it runs;
        a joined page that ticks spectate (or spectator 1 at its console) only watches: no body, a noclip move through the level, nothing hurts it and the monsters ignore it, its attack key chases a player (3.20's chase cam: the view behind that player's), jump lets go, chase n at its console picks one; its console's game commands (god, give, kill…) run at the host for its player; at the host's console players lists the clients and what drives each and kick n drops a guest;
        two players whose networks a STUN server cannot cross give both pages a TURN relay (rtc_ice turn:host:3478 user pass at the console, kept in the settings; rtc_ice none drops it);
        a relay of your own for the texts (npm run signal in the repository, rtc_signal http://host:8787 at the console of both pages; from this https page an http relay is reached at localhost only, elsewhere put it behind https) lets Host show a four-letter code instead, typed after Join or given to connect at the console (disconnect leaves, reconnect joins the last host again: Host again keeps the host's code).
        The page opens on the attract loop, the pak's recording with the main menu over it, and goes back to it after a game; a new game starts from the menu, the Map select or the console.
        The Demo select (or demomap q2demo1 at the console) plays a recording too; a key or a click brings the menu up over it; timedemo q2demo1 plays it as fast as the page paints and reports the frames a second, screenshot downloads the view as quake00.png.
        record name at the console records the game from then on as a demo of its own, stop (or the level's end) downloads it as name.dm2, which the select plays here and Quake 2 plays itself with the same pak (a joined page records the frames the host sends it the same way);
        the recordings are kept across reloads (demos lists them, deldemo name drops one), and a .dm2 file of your own opens from the Demo select's file button.

How it works

      
    
        
  • The BSP becomes tables. A Quake 2 map (IBSP 38) is copied in lump by lump: FACES with their
          plane and texture mapping, FACE_VERTS (edges and surfedges resolved), NODES,
          LEAVES with their cluster and a decompressed PVS as a hex string, LEAFFACES, and the
          collision geometry Quake 2 ships instead of clipnodes: BRUSHES and BRUSHSIDES with their
          planes and contents, indexed through LEAFBRUSHES. The MD2 models contribute MODELS and
          their frame runs in ANIMS; the entity lump becomes MAP_ENTS.
    • 
        
  • Collision is a recursive procedure. RHC is CM_RecursiveHullCheck: the moving box's
          extents push each split plane out, the segment is split at the plane and both sides are visited nearest first; in
          every leaf reached, CLIP_LEAF clips the segment against each brush whose contents match the mask
          (CM_ClipBoxToBrush). TRACE_MOVE runs it against the world and every door and platform
          (rotating doors are traced in their own rotated space) and tests monsters and the player with a Minkowski slab
          test. FLY_MOVE, WALK_MOVE (the 18-unit step), MOVE_STEP and
          PUSH_MOVE are g_phys.c in PSQL.
    • 
        
  • The game is PSQL. Q2_TIC runs at 20 Hz: pmove's ducking, friction, acceleration, jumping and swimming, and the view's bob;
          doors and their teams, rotating doors, platforms, buttons, trains with path corners, rotating fans, timers;
          triggers (once, multiple, relay, always, key, counter, hurt, push), targets (speaker, explosion, splash, secret,
          goal, help, laser, changelevel), items from stimpacks to the power shield, the ten weapons from the blaster to
          the BFG10K, damage with armour and quad, gibs, and the monster AI of g_ai.c for the guards, enforcers, gunners,
          berserkers, flyers, parasites and the tank (and, as logic without their models, the full game's gladiators, iron
          maidens, brains, medics, mutants, icaruses, technicians, sharks, the supertank, the hornet, Jorg and Makron).
    • 
        
  • The renderer is a query. FRAME_ALL finds the leaf and cluster the eye is in and, once per
          cluster, marks every face of every leaf whose cluster is in its PVS (Quake's visframe) with its plane and bounding
          sphere. A frame is then a scan of the marked faces with the back-face and frustum tests as expressions, aggregated
          with LIST() into one row of face ids; doors, platforms and the fan are brush models and add a row each
          at their own origin. The same result set carries the alias models and sprites in the PVS with their pose and
          frame, the light styles, the sounds and effects of the tic: one round trip per frame. The other renderer mode,
          FRAME_FACES, projects every vertex in SQL as well.
    • 
        
  • JavaScript only paints. It rasterises the polygons with perspective-correct spans over a surface cache
          (the .wal texture × the lightmap, through colormap.pcx, as ref_soft did), blends translucent surfaces through the
          alpha map, draws the MD2 models with Gouraud light sampled from the lightmap below them, the sky box, particles,
          and the status bar from pics/. Sounds are rows in SOUND_EVENTS, spatialised with the Web Audio API;
          the map's looped speakers play at their origins. The soundtrack was CD audio: drop track02.ogg…
          into music/ (or pick the folder: its tracks are kept as they play) and each map plays its worldspawn track; without them a synthesised drone fills in.
    • 
      

      
A port of Firebird Quake to Quake 2. The engine is
        Electric Firebird (firebird-wasm). The game
        data is the Quake 2 demo (id Software, 1997), which may be freely redistributed; drop in a registered
        pak0.pak to play the full game's base unit. Quake 2 is a trademark of id Software.
